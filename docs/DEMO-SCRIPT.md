# Rhubarb demo video: script and shot list

Target length **2:50** (the rule is under 3:00). English voice-over; Japanese lines get English
subtitles. Public on YouTube. Every app shot comes from the **real Rhubarb** on Railway, never the
Vercel demo: the Bee track requires real Bee data doing something for the user.

Voice-over is about 330 words. Read it at an easy pace and it lands near 2:40, leaving room for
the Japanese lines to breathe.

---

## Before you film: make these true first

Do these over the next few days, in this order. Each one is a shot you'll need.

1. **Say yes to the tax-free plan.** In Learning, open "免税 shopping: asking for tax-free at the
   register", tap **Yes**, choose **Know some** and your reading help. Wait for the lesson
   (a few minutes; your Bee buzzes).
2. **Do the lesson once,** including the quiz. On the "Teach it back" step, tap **Send to my
   Bee** so a practice alarm comes to your phone. Answer it out loud into your Bee.
3. **Use it for real.** At a shop, ask 「免税できますか」 and follow wherever they send you.
   Within about 15 minutes Rhubarb should credit it: a phone alarm "Heard you use…", a credit in
   Looking back, the honeycomb cell fills a little. **This is the money shot. Don't film until
   it has happened once.** If it doesn't come through, tell the build session: it can check what
   Bee actually heard (a misheard 免税 is the likely culprit) and why it didn't count.
4. **Look over a day review** in the evening (Your day, then "Look it over").
5. **One "Hey Rhubarb" ask** somewhere scenic, e.g. "Hey Rhubarb, teach me how the Osaka Loop
   Line got its name." Optional, but it's the most memorable 15 seconds.

## Filming rules (keep it fair to the people around you)

- **Don't show shop staff's faces.** Film your hands, your phone, the street, the sign. Bee
  captures the conversation as text; the video doesn't need their face.
- Phone screen: iPhone screen recording (Control Centre). App screens: QuickTime on the Mac,
  "New Screen Recording", browser zoom 125%, one tab, light mode.
- Have someone film the street shots from behind or the side, or prop the phone up.

## The script

### 0:00 – 0:15 · Hook
**Shot:** Osaka street, close-up of the Bee on your wrist, then you walking into a shop.
**VO:**
> I'm learning Japanese in Osaka. Every day I have exactly the conversations I'm trying to learn,
> and by dinner they're gone. Rhubarb listens through my Bee, turns my own day into lessons, and
> then hears me get better.

**On screen (title card, 2 s):** Rhubarb · It hears you get better.

### 0:15 – 0:38 · The real moment
**Shot:** Your POV at the counter (hands, phone, the TAX FREE sign, no staff faces).
**Audio:** your real exchange. Subtitle both lines.
> You: 「タックスフリーで買いたいんですけど…」 *(I'd like to buy tax-free…)*
> Clerk: 「1階の免税カウンターで聞いてみてください」 *(Please ask at the tax-free counter on the first floor.)*
**VO (over a beat of you looking lost):**
> I got the question out. The answer went right past me.

### 0:38 – 1:02 · It checks before it assumes
**Shot:** Mac. Your day → the review banner → "Look it over" → the tax-free finding → tap
**Right**. Then a finding with a misheard word → **Not quite** → type the correction.
**VO:**
> That evening, Rhubarb shows me what it thinks happened. Speech recognition mishears, and it
> can't tell my voice from anyone else's, so it asks me first. I confirm what's true, fix what it
> got wrong, and nothing gets built on a mishearing.

### 1:02 – 1:32 · A lesson made from my own words
**Shot:** Learning → the tax-free plan → open the lesson. Linger on: the card quoting the
clerk's actual sentence; the floors card (1階 いっかい, 3階 さんがい); "What was checked" with the
fact-check line.
**VO:**
> Then it writes a ten-minute lesson, built around that exact sentence: what she said, how floors
> are counted, what happens at the counter. A second AI reads it for factual mistakes before I
> ever see it, and anything it isn't sure of, it tells me.

### 1:32 – 1:52 · Practice out loud
**Shot:** iPhone, locked screen: the Bee alarm arrives ("Explain it out loud…"). You hold the
Bee and answer in Japanese. Cut to the feedback on the phone or in the app.
**VO:**
> Practice comes to my phone as an alarm. I answer out loud into the Bee, and it tells me what I
> got and the one thing to add.

### 1:52 – 2:22 · It hears me get better (the core of the pitch)
**Shot:** Street → a different shop → you say 「免税できますか」 and head to the counter (no faces).
Then the phone buzzes: "Heard you use…". Cut to the Mac: Looking back shows **Used it for real**,
then Learning → the honeycomb cell for tax-free filling.
**VO:**
> Two days later, at a different shop, I ask for tax-free myself and follow where they send me.
> Rhubarb hears it. Not a quiz, not an app streak. Real Japanese, used for real, and it counts
> that as progress.

### 2:22 – 2:37 · Ask for anything out loud (optional, cut if long)
**Shot:** On the Tempozan ferris wheel, you say "Hey Rhubarb, teach me…". Cut to the lesson
waiting on the Mac.
**VO:**
> And when I'm curious, I just ask. By the time I'm back on the ground, the lesson is waiting.

### 2:37 – 2:50 · How it's built, and trust
**Shot:** Settings → Privacy and safety, then the GitHub repo.
**VO:**
> It keeps text, never audio. Private moments are dropped before any AI sees them, and I can
> forget a moment, a day, or everything. Rhubarb runs on Bee's live stream and CLI with Claude,
> and it's open source.

**End card (3 s):** Rhubarb · github.com/OJ-IRO/rhubarb

## If you're over time, cut in this order

1. The "Hey Rhubarb" ferris wheel beat (2:22 – 2:37).
2. The misheard-word correction in the review (keep the Right tap).
3. Shorten the practice beat to the alarm arriving plus one spoken line.

Never cut the real-world credit (1:52 – 2:22). It is the one thing no other entry has.
