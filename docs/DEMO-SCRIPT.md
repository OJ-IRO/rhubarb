# Rhubarb demo video: the Ferris wheel cut

Target length **2:30** (the rule is under 3:00). Opens on your own footage from the Tempozan
Ferris wheel; everything else is a screen recording of your **real Rhubarb** on Railway, with a
voice-over recorded last. English voice-over; English subtitles on anything said in Japanese.

The Bee track's hard rule: the video must show the project using **real data from a real Bee**.
Your wheel footage plus your real Rhubarb screens do exactly that. Never record the Vercel demo.

---

## The script

### 0:00 – 0:15 · On the wheel
**Screen:** your footage from the Tempozan wheel. Get the Bee on your wrist in frame if you can.
**Voice-over:**
> I'm on the Tempozan Ferris wheel in Osaka, wearing my Bee. I want to know how this thing got
> here, so I just ask out loud.

### 0:15 – 0:25 · The ask
**Screen:** keep the footage; if it has your voice asking, let it play with a subtitle.
**Voice-over (only if your clip has no audio):**
> "Hey Bee, give me a lesson on the history of the Ferris wheel, and this one in particular."

### 0:25 – 0:50 · Bee mishears, I fix it
**Screen:** Learning → "The history of the Ferris wheel, from Chicago to Osaka" → scroll to
"Why it's here" → the moment with the **corrected** tag → open **Fix what was said** so the
conversation lines show, then close it.
**Voice-over:**
> Rhubarb heard me through the Bee. But Bee heard "Osaka" as "Old Southfield", and a lesson
> about the wrong wheel is worse than none. So every moment can be fixed. I corrected one word,
> and Rhubarb rewrote the lesson from what I really said.

### 0:50 – 1:20 · The lesson
**Screen:** open the lesson. Linger on: the cover "Why there's a wheel here at all", then
"You asked from inside the thing" (your own words), the diagram "How the idea travelled", then
"Which one were you on". End on "What was checked".
**Voice-over:**
> By the time I'm back on the ground, the lesson's waiting. It starts with my own question,
> asked from inside the wheel, then tells the whole story: Chicago, Osaka, and which wheel I was
> actually on. Every quote is checked against what Bee really heard, and the sources are real.

### 1:20 – 1:45 · It checks before it assumes
**Screen:** Your day → **Look it over** → tap **Right** on one finding → **Not quite** on another
and type a short correction → Done.
**Voice-over:**
> Most of my day isn't a question I ask out loud. So every evening, Rhubarb shows me what it
> thinks happened. Bee can mishear, and it can't tell my voice from anyone else's, so it asks
> first. Nothing gets built on a guess.

### 1:45 – 2:10 · My real day becomes my Japanese plan
**Screen:** Learning → the plans from your real days (tax-free at the register, station
announcements, phone calls with delivery drivers) → tap **Yes** on one → pick **Know some** and
the reading help. If you have it, the practice alarm on your phone.
**Voice-over:**
> From what I confirm, it suggests what to learn: tax-free at a register, station announcements
> in Hakata, a delivery driver on the phone. I say yes, say how much I already know, and it
> pitches every lesson to me. Practice comes back through the Bee, out loud.

### 2:10 – 2:30 · Trust, and how it's built
**Screen:** Settings → Privacy and safety. End on the GitHub repo page.
**Voice-over:**
> It keeps text, never audio. Private moments are dropped before any AI sees them, and I can
> forget a moment, a day, or everything. Rhubarb is built on Bee's live stream and CLI with
> Claude, and it's open source.

**End card (3 s):** Rhubarb · It hears you get better · github.com/OJ-IRO/rhubarb

## Before you record

- [ ] There's a day review waiting (Your day shows the banner), or tap "Review this day now" on
      a recent day an hour before recording.
- [ ] Mac: QuickTime → File → New Screen Recording, browser zoom 125%, one tab, light mode,
      notifications off. Move slowly; pause on each screen.
- [ ] Voice-over recorded last, then laid over the clips (iMovie or CapCut).

## If it runs long, cut in this order

1. The plans beat: keep the Yes tap, drop the practice alarm.
2. The "Not quite" correction in the review (keep the Right tap).

Keep the wheel, the fix, and the lesson. They are one real story, start to finish.
