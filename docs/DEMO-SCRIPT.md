# Rhubarb demo video

Target length **2:30** (the rule is under 3:00). A screen recording of your **real Rhubarb** on
Railway, with a voice-over recorded last, plus one short clip of your own footage from the
Ferris wheel. English voice-over; English subtitles on anything said in Japanese.

The Bee track's hard rule: the video must show the project using **real data from a real Bee**.
Never record the Vercel demo.

The main story is learning Japanese from your real day. The Ferris wheel is one short example
of the other way to use it: ask about anything, wherever you are.

---

## The script

### 0:00 – 0:15 · The problem
**Screen:** Your day, on a real day with lots heard (Oct 1 in Hakata or Oct 6).
**Voice-over:**
> I'm learning Japanese while living in Japan. Every day I have the exact conversations I'm
> trying to learn from, and by dinner they're gone. So I built a tutor that listens through my Bee.

### 0:15 – 0:35 · What Bee heard
**Screen:** the same day: how much was heard, the moments it noticed, where (Hakata, Fukuoka).
**Voice-over:**
> Rhubarb reads the Bee's live transcript, text only, never audio. Out of a whole day it notices
> the moments worth learning from: a clerk I didn't follow, a station announcement, something I
> tried to say.

### 0:35 – 1:00 · It checks before it assumes
**Screen:** Your day → **Look it over** → tap **Right** on one finding → **Not quite** on another
and type a short correction → Done.
**Voice-over:**
> But Bee can mishear, and it can't tell my voice from anyone else's. So every evening, Rhubarb
> shows me what it thinks happened, and I mark it right or not quite. Nothing gets built on a
> guess.

### 1:00 – 1:20 · My day becomes my plan
**Screen:** Learning → the plans from your real days (tax-free at the register, station
announcements, delivery drivers on the phone) → tap **Yes** on one → pick **Know some** and the
reading help.
**Voice-over:**
> From what I confirm, it suggests what to learn. I say yes, say how much I already know, and how
> much reading help I want.

### 1:20 – 1:45 · The lesson, and practice
**Screen:** open a Japanese lesson (tax-free, if it's written by then). Linger on the card with
your own quoted moment, then "What was checked". Then the practice alarm on your phone.
**Voice-over:**
> The lesson is built around my own words, like the exact line the clerk said to me. A second AI
> checks every fact before I see it. Then practice comes to my phone, and I answer out loud into
> the Bee.

### 1:45 – 2:10 · Or just ask about anything
**Screen:** 5 seconds of your Ferris wheel footage → the Ferris wheel plan → the **corrected**
moment → flick through two or three lesson cards.
**Voice-over:**
> It's not only for Japanese. On the Ferris wheel in Osaka, I asked how it got there. Bee heard
> "Osaka" as "Old Southfield", so I fixed one word, and the lesson about that wheel was waiting
> when I got down.

### 2:10 – 2:30 · Trust, and how it's built
**Screen:** Settings → Privacy and safety. End on the GitHub repo page.
**Voice-over:**
> Private moments are dropped before any AI sees them, and I can forget a moment, a day, or
> everything. Rhubarb is built on Bee's live stream and CLI with Claude, and it's open source.

**End card (3 s):** Rhubarb · It hears you get better · github.com/OJ-IRO/rhubarb

## Before you record

- [ ] There's a day review waiting (Your day shows the banner), or tap "Review this day now" on
      a recent day an hour before recording.
- [ ] If you've set up Bee voice recognition by then, cut "it can't tell my voice from anyone
      else's" from the review beat.
- [ ] Mac: QuickTime → File → New Screen Recording, browser zoom 125%, one tab, light mode,
      notifications off. Move slowly; pause on each screen.
- [ ] Voice-over recorded last, then laid over the clips (iMovie or CapCut).

## If it runs long, cut in this order

1. The practice alarm (keep the lesson).
2. The "Not quite" correction in the review (keep the Right tap).
3. The Ferris wheel footage (keep the corrected moment on screen).
